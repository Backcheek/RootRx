The Plant Cards

Ashkibwaan

Jerusalem Artichoke

Helianthus tuberosus L.

Habitat: Moist soils across eastern Canada and northwestern US. Grows 5 to 10 feet tall with rough branching stems and golden-yellow autumn flower heads.

Traditional Uses: The iron-rich, sweet, nut-like edible tubers contain simple carbohydrates that convert into natural sugars without raising glucose levels, making them excellent dietary staples for managing diabetes.

Modern Drugstore Match

Metformin (Glucophage)

Both are effective tools for managing blood sugar levels in people with diabetes. While the natural tuber contains unique carbohydrates that will not raise glucose, prescription Metformin actively controls blood sugar and helps the body use insulin efficiently.

Doodooshaboojiibik

Common Dandelion

Taraxacum officinale

Habitat: Highly ubiquitous perennial found all over North American yards, fields, and roadsides, standing up to 20 inches tall with a hollow, milky stalk.

Traditional Uses: Virtually every part holds value: young leaves are eaten raw in salads, crowns are steamed as hot vegetables, and roasted roots act as coffee. Used to remedy gallbladder problems, urinary disorders, high blood pressure, and gout.

Modern Drugstore Match

Cholestyramine

Both address issues related to the gallbladder and liver system. Cholestyramine works by removing excess bile acids from the body to treat liver-related problems, mirroring the dandelion's traditional use as a remedy for the gallbladder—an organ intimately tied to bile storage and digestive health.

Ginebigo-ojiibik

Seneca Snakeroot

Polygala senega

Habitat: Prefers high-altitude rocky or limestone environments from New England to the Canadian Rockies. Stands 0.5 to 2 feet tall with white/pink spikes.

Traditional Uses: Historically treated snakebites, colds, sore throats, toothaches, and earaches. Modern applications deploy it as a powerful cough syrup expectorant and throat gargle [acts as a laxative or emetic in high doses].

Modern Drugstore Match

Guaifenesin

Both function to relieve respiratory symptoms associated with deep colds. While Guaifenesin thins and clears heavy mucus and phlegm from the chest during a cold or flu, Seneca Snakeroot serves the exact same purpose by helping the body expel respiratory secretions to ease coughing.

Makizin

Yellow Lady's Slipper

Cypripedium parviflorum

Habitat: Specialized woodland and bog environments, growing up to 31.25 inches with strongly ribbed leaves and a specialized pouch-shaped flower to enforce insect cross-pollination.

Traditional Uses: Critically revered in Ojibway legend. Its unpleasantly scented roots function as an antispasmodic, sedative, and nerve tonic to treat severe anxiety, tension, and sleep issues. Requires alcohol tincture extraction.

Modern Drugstore Match

Alprazolam (Xanax)

Both function to calm the body's nervous system and relieve psychological tension. Alprazolam is a prescription medication designed to treat anxiety by helping the nervous system calm down, sharing its core therapeutic goal with these traditional root extracts used for severe tension and sleep loss.

Manomin

Wild Rice

Zizania aquatica

Habitat: Large aquatic grass growing 10 to 12 feet tall in shallow, slow-moving waters across eastern North America, ripening into dark purple seed-heavy spikes by autumn.

Traditional Uses: A vital regional dietary staple for First Nations. Seeds are boiled like rice, ground into meal for thickening soups and baking bread, providing a dense supply of essential riboflavin and niacin.

Modern Drugstore Match

Vitamin B-Complex Tablets

Both serve as excellent options for delivering essential B vitamins to the human body. Pharmacy tablets are designed to prevent low vitamin B levels to support vital functions like metabolism and energy, while wild rice is a dense, natural food staple that naturally provides a rich supply of riboflavin and niacin.

Mayagi Mashkosiw

Big Bluestem

Andropogon gerardii

Habitat: Dominant prairie grass reaching 2 to 6 feet in height, featuring incredibly deep roots extending down to 12 feet to survive severe droughts. Favors fertile, dry lowland soils.

Traditional Uses: Forms dense clumps of long, flat leaves used historically in decoctions to manage high fevers and painful digestive tract disorders.

Modern Drugstore Match

Acetaminophen (Tylenol)

Both function to alleviate physical pain and reduce high fevers. While Acetaminophen works to temporarily reduce a fever and elevate the body's overall pain threshold, a decoction of these flat grass leaves has traditionally served as a direct remedy to bring down body temperature and soothe internal distress.

Mazaanaatig

Canada Thistle

Cirsium arvense

Habitat: Introduced by Europeans, thrives aggressively in disturbed lands, roadsides, and pastures. Highly branched, smooth-stemmed, standing 1 to 5 feet with spiny leaves.

Traditional Uses: Its deep roots have historically been prepared as astringents, diuretics, general tonics, and remedies for localized toothaches. The soft, fuzzy material on its seeds provides exceptional tinder for fire-starting.

Modern Drugstore Match

Hydrochlorothiazide

Both function as active diuretics to help eliminate fluid from the body. Hydrochlorothiazide is a water pill prescribed to increase the flow of urine to reduce excess water for fluid retention or high blood pressure, sharing this specific fluid-clearing therapeutic property with the deep roots of this thistle.

Meskojiibikak

Bloodroot

Sanguinaria canadensis

Habitat: Low-growing perennial of eastern North American woodlands, recognizable by waxy-white early spring blossoms and a single deeply lobed grayish-green leaf.

Traditional Uses: Named for the vibrant red juice found in its rootstock. Used as a dye, a tool for divination, and a remedy for fevers and rheumatism. Modernly used as an expectorant for congestion. Contains dangerous, toxic opium-like alkaloids.

Modern Drugstore Match

Codeine

Both possess highly active, potent chemical properties that impact the central nervous system, while carrying significant risks of toxicity. Just as prescription Codeine acts directly on the central nervous system to alter pain and cough responses, bloodroot contains powerful, dangerous opium-like alkaloids.

Mitigomizh

Bur Oak

Quercus macrocarpa

Habitat: Massive, widely distributed tree exceeding 100 feet in height with deeply ridged bark and unique acorns half-covered by warty, long-fringed caps ripening in fall.

Traditional Uses: Versatile tree: wood was crafted into bows and spears, nut meat processed into pastes, and inner bark brewed into medicinal teas for chronic diarrhea and heart issues.

Modern Drugstore Match

Bismuth Subsalicylate (Pepto-Bismol)

Both function as reliable remedies to treat and manage diarrhea. Bismuth subsalicylate is used to actively resolve diarrhea and calm related symptoms of an upset stomach, serving the exact same therapeutic purpose as the tree's inner bark when brewed into traditional digestive teas.

Wild Ginger

Asarum canadense

Habitat: Low-growing ground cover in rich woods, featuring pairs of large, hairy, heart-shaped leaves that canopy a single ground-level reddish-brown flower.

Traditional Uses: Gives off a pungent ginger aroma, roots can be boiled with sugar into candy. Medicinally used to ease whooping cough, heal wounds, and settle stomachs. Chewed by local sturgeon fishermen to prevent boats from flipping.

Modern Drugstore Match

Antacid Tablets

Both function to soothe digestive discomfort and quickly settle the stomach. Antacids work by neutralizing excess stomach acid to relieve heartburn and indigestion, matching the common goal of wild ginger roots which have traditionally been deployed to ease gastrointestinal distress.

Niibaa'obag

Closed Gentian

Gentiana andrewsii

Habitat: Damp fields and open forest borders from Quebec to North Carolina. Grows 24 inches tall with tightly closed, cup-shaped blue-to-violet flowers.

Traditional Uses: The intensely bitter root has a long history of traditional use as an appetite stimulant, general tonic, laxative, and cathartic used to treat weak stomachs.

Modern Drugstore Match

Metoclopramide (Reglan)

Both function to stimulate the digestive tract and assist a weak or slow stomach. Metoclopramide helps muscles move food along to relieve full or frozen symptoms, sharing its therapeutic purpose with this intensely bitter root traditionally used to encourage proper movement and function.

Nookwezigan

Yarrow

Achillea millefolium

Habitat: Highly prevalent perennial in disturbed ditches and fields. Standing 1 to 3 feet tall with gray-green hairy stems and stalkless, fern-like fragrant leaves.

Traditional Uses: Crushed leaves stop bleeding instantly when applied directly to cuts. Steeped into a blood-purifying tea to treat fevers, colds, stomach cramps, and headaches.

Modern Drugstore Match

Ibuprofen (Advil)

Both function to effectively reduce high fevers and relieve internal bodily pain. While Ibuprofen targets mild to moderate pain, joint stiffness, and cramps, yarrow leaves have traditionally been steeped into a medicinal tea specifically designed to target fevers, stomach cramps, and headaches.

Ode'iminidjiibik

Wild Strawberry

Fragaria virginiana

Habitat: Low-growing ground plant across North America, spreading via thin running stems, three-parted toothed leaves, and small five-petaled white spring flowers.

Traditional Uses: The sweet fruit holds immense cultural significance. Tea prepared from the leaves treats severe cholera and stimulates standard appetite.

Modern Drugstore Match

Pedialyte

Both serve as remedies to treat severe, dehydrating gastrointestinal illnesses. Pedialyte is formulated to rapidly restore fluids and electrolytes lost through vomiting or diarrhea, acting on the same goals as traditional strawberry leaf teas historically used to combat the severe effects of cholera.

Omakakiibag

Pale Touch-Me-Not / Jewelweed

Impatiens pallida

Habitat: Tall, translucent-stemmed plant of wet woods and shaded areas, featuring dangling pale yellow flowers and fragile seed capsules that burst violently when ripe.

Traditional Uses: The soothing liquid sap is applied externally as an immediate remedy for ringworm, warts, hemorrhoids, and chemical irritation from poison ivy or stinging nettles.

Modern Drugstore Match

Hydrocortisone Cream

Both function as active topical remedies used to soothe painful skin irritation and reduce inflammation. While commercial hydrocortisone cream is designed to decrease swelling and itching from rashes, the soothing liquid sap of this plant acts as an immediate natural shield against poison ivy or nettles.

Sasapkwanins

Wild Bergamot

Monarda fistulosa

Habitat: Fragrant, 2-to-4-foot-tall herbaceous perennial found in dry woods, thickets, and forest clearings east of the Rockies, producing two-lipped tubular lavender flowers.

Traditional Uses: Aromatic leaves are dried and steeped into a potent, minty therapeutic tea valued as an insect repellent and a remedy for severe digestive complications and external burns.

Modern Drugstore Match

Bacitracin Ointment

Both function as topical remedies utilized to treat and manage skin burns. Bacitracin is an antibiotic medication applied to the skin to prevent and treat infections in minor cuts or burns, matching the historical application of dried bergamot leaves which were brewed into a therapeutic healing wash for external burns.

Wabos'odji'bik

Wild Sarsaparilla

Aralia nudicaulis

Habitat: 19-inch-tall perennial of mixed and hardwood forests, featuring a stout woody stem splitting into a single leaf of 3-5 leaflets and a separate leafless stalk.

Traditional Uses: Sweet rhizome used to brew early root beer. Medicinally deployed to soothe chronic coughs, heal fresh cuts, treat ulcers, and manage painful shingles outbreaks.

Modern Drugstore Match

Acyclovir

Both are actively utilized to treat and manage the pain of shingles outbreaks. Acyclovir is a specialized prescription antiviral designed to relieve discomfort and make viral skin sores heal faster, which runs completely parallel to the traditional medicinal deployment of this sweet wild rhizome.

Wezaawinagak

Canada Goldenrod

Solidago canadensis

Habitat: Tall, hairy-stemmed plant growing up to 5 feet high across North America, showcasing prominent veins on lanceolate leaves and tiny yellow flower clusters on arching branches.

Traditional Uses: Frequently falsely blamed for hay fever. Crushed roots or flower poultices were traditionally applied to alleviate painful boils, burns, and ulcers, leaves serve as an organic origin for rubber extraction.

Modern Drugstore Match

Silver Sulfadiazine Cream

Both function as active topical treatments to alleviate and heal painful burns. Silver sulfadiazine is a clinical cream applied to prevent wound infections in second- and third-degree burn patients, sharing its exact protective and soothing goals with these traditional flower poultices.

Wiigwaas

White Birch

Betula papyrifera

Habitat: Medium-sized hardwood tree with a signature white peeling trunk bark as it matures, thriving best in sandy, well-drained soils but tolerating bogs.

Traditional Uses: Sap is boiled into syrup, inner bark acts as emergency food. Outer bark fashioned into canoes and baskets, resin acts as a disinfectant for stomach pain.

Modern Drugstore Match

Charcoal Tablets

Both function as internal remedies to treat and alleviate deep stomach discomfort. While charcoal capsules are commonly used to treat gastrointestinal gas, pressure, and bloating in the digestive tract, white birch tree resin has historically been relied upon as a disinfectant to soothe stomach pain.

Wiinizikens

Panicled Aster

Symphyotrichum lanceolatum

Habitat: Tall plant [4 to 5 feet] producing a massive spread of white flower heads with pinkish-yellow centers from August to September in damp ditches.

Traditional Uses: Prepared as a healing infusion alongside other flora to reduce raging fevers, boiled into a decoction to wash open wounds, or dried into a powdered salve for skin abrasions.

Modern Drugstore Match

Acetaminophen (Tylenol)

Both function to successfully reduce high fevers and treat physical aches or open wounds. Acetaminophen helps the body eliminate excess heat and elevates your pain threshold, mimicking how this aster was prepared as an infusion to lower raging fevers and clean skin abrasions.

Wild Licorice Root

Glycyrrhiza lepidota

Habitat: 1-to-3-foot-tall plant found in moist, sandy prairies and stream valleys, featuring sticky stems, opposite leaves, alfalfa-like cream flowers.

Traditional Uses: Fleshy root contains glycyrrhizin [50x sweeter than sugar]. Edible roots and shoots. Historically deployed to treat deep skin sores, toothaches, and foot ailments.

Modern Drugstore Match

Benzocaine Gel

Both function to relieve localized physical pain, specifically in the mouth and teeth. Benzocaine is a local anesthetic that numbs targeted tissue to relieve minor pain and irritation in the throat or gums, sharing its exact function with this sweet fleshy root historically used to treat toothaches.

Zesabiins

Indian Hemp

Apocynum cannabinum

Habitat: Sturdy, erect plant standing 3 to 4 feet tall with a purplish stem, opposite leaves with a white plum-like coating, and seed pods in disturbed roadsides.

Traditional Uses: Incredibly strong fibers woven into durable twine for fishing nets and bowstrings. Used historically to treat coughs, but the plant contains dangerous cardio-toxins and must never be consumed.

Modern Drugstore Match

Digoxin (Lanoxin)

Both substances contain highly advanced, powerful chemical compounds that directly affect heart function. While prescription Digoxin stabilizes heart rhythm to help it pump blood more efficiently, Indian hemp naturally contains dangerous, intense cardio-toxins that impact the cardiovascular system.
