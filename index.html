The Big Ideas Behind the Medicine

Mind and Body as One

In traditional healing, there is no split between the body and the spirit. To have a healthy body, you need a healthy spirit. Healing is about fixing physical issues while taking care of the spiritual side of life.

The Doctrine of Signatures

This is the belief that the way a plant looks [like its shape or color] gives you a clue about what it cures. Both European settlers and Native Americans shared this way of looking at nature.

Sharing and Blending

Over the last 500 years, Native and European healing methods blended together a lot. In fact, early European settlers only survived because they learned which local plants to use as medicine from Indigenous people.

Disclaimer

The healing power of a plant comes from natural mixtures inside it [like alkaloids and tannins]. These mixtures change depending on the plant's life cycle, its genes, and the environment. Because of this, traditional healers use strict rules and cleansing routines to handle them safely.

A Look Back at History

Health Before European Contact

Early Native Americans were incredibly healthy people. Old skeletons show almost no signs of things like tooth decay, arthritis, or weak bones. Before Europeans arrived, people often lived up to 100 years thanks to their active lifestyle and natural medicine.

The Disease Crisis

Because the Americas and Europe were separated for thousands of years, people developed differently. Native Americans lived in smaller groups and had never been exposed to European germs. When new diseases like smallpox arrived, they had no natural defense against them, which led to a massive loss of life.

Adapting to New Illnesses

Traditional medicine did not stop, it changed quickly to try and treat new sicknesses like smallpox, measles, and tuberculosis. For example, stories talk about using a plant called Devil's club to help with diabetes, which became a common problem in the community.

A Shared Approach

Around 300 years ago, European medicine and Native medicine were actually very similar. Both sides relied heavily on the exact same things, including warm herbal mud packs [poultices], herbal teas [infusions], boiled bark drinks [decoctions], and skin ointments. Plus, both systems used prayer as a main part of the cure.
